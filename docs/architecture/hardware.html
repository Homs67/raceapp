Data sources

Three pieces of hardware feed the app. Each has its own driver, its own quirks, and a different idea of what "accurate" means. This page covers what each provides, and — the part that trips people up — which one wins when two of them report the same thing.

The iPhone

Packages/SessionKit/…/PhoneSensorSuite.swift owns CLLocationManager, CMMotionManager and CMAltimeter, and publishes everything through a single (channel, value, t) callback that AppModel points at the bus.

  • GPS runs at the best accuracy iOS allows. Each fix is published with its own accuracy (gps.hAcc, gps.vAcc, gps.speedAcc) — the "honesty channels" that let later analysis decide how much to trust a position.
  • Motion runs at 100 Hz and drops to 20 Hz when the app is backgrounded during a recording, to stay clear of thermal suspension.
  • Car-frame G is also computed here when no RaceBox is attached: the phone's axes are arbitrary (it sits in a cradle at any angle), so a calibrator learns which way is down and which way is forward. See Motion → car-frame G.
Background recording

Recording continues with the screen locked thanks to the location and bluetooth-central background modes. Without Always location permission iOS may suspend the app, so the recorder falls back to keeping the screen awake for the session.

OBD-II adapter

A cheap Bluetooth ELM327 clone (the reference is the Veepeak OBDCheck BLE+, deliberately the slowest common adapter). The app speaks to it as a serial line over BLE.

Polling mode (default)

ConnectionController drives the lifecycle — scan, connect, discover GATT, initialise the ELM, reach the ECU — and then hands the link to PidPoller:

  • Fast loop, polled continuously: RPM, speed, throttle, accelerator pedal.
  • Slow loop, one channel per window, a full sweep every 5 s: coolant, intake/ambient temp, fuel level, module voltage, engine load, timing advance, MAP, MAF, barometric pressure. Oil temp is in the list but the ND2's ECU doesn't support it.

Decoding is pure: PidDecoder turns raw response bytes into engineering values per SAE J1979, and is fully unit-tested.

CAN monitor mode

OBD-II only exposes what the ECU chooses to answer. Steering angle, brake pedal and a fast RPM only exist as broadcast frames on the car's CAN bus. With Settings → Record CAN signals on, starting a recording switches the adapter into ELM327 monitor mode (ATMA) and decodes those frames instead of polling.

Trade-offs you should know
  • A cheap ELM can only filter one frame ID at a time, so the stream round-robins between IDs. On a real drive RPM was captured 44% of the time, steering and brake about 15%.
  • Polling and monitoring can't run at once on one adapter. Slow PIDs are meant to be read in a ~1.5 s interlude every 60 s — but on the last real drive that interlude returned nothing, so no obd.* data was recorded at all. Tracked on Status.
  • CAN RPM and pedal are mirrored onto obd.rpm / obd.throttle with publishLive — live display only, never recorded — so the recorded data stays honest about where each number came from.

RaceBox

A dedicated GNSS logger: 25 Hz position and speed with real accuracy figures, plus a 3-axis accelerometer and gyro. It is plugged into the OBD port (the Micro has no battery and runs from 12 V or USB), so — unlike the phone — it doesn't move.

  • Protocol: u-blox UBX-style binary packets over BLE, transcribed from RaceBox's protocol document (rev 9) in Packages/RaceBoxKit. Notifications arrive split across MTU-sized chunks; RaceBoxPacketParser reassembles them and verifies checksums.
  • Models differ: the Mini reports battery percent and has no memory; the Mini S and Micro can record on their own. The Micro reports its input voltage instead of a battery level. Commands are gated by RaceBoxModel capabilities.
  • Stored memory: Mini S / Micro hold one continuous log. Settings → RaceBox → Recorded on device downloads it and splits it into separate sessions. See Pipeline → Importing RaceBox memory.
  • Auto-connect: a remembered device reconnects at launch and on foreground, without scanning.

Two Bluetooth links at once

Both devices talk through Packages/BleKit. The design decision that makes simultaneous connections possible: one CoreBluetoothTransport — and therefore one CBCentralManager — per device profile, each with its own state-restoration identifier.

Who wins when sources overlap

The RaceBox and the phone both measure position, speed and acceleration. Mixing them is worse than using either: a 25 Hz ±0.3 m stream with a 1 Hz ±10 m fix dropped into it means anything reading "the latest value" gets the bad one some of the time. So each canonical channel has exactly one owner at a time.

Rule

Ownership is time-based, not a flag: a source owns a channel while its samples are fresh. If the RaceBox drops out mid-drive, the phone takes over within three seconds on its own — no code has to remember to hand back. The session manifest records which source drove gps.* over time in motionSourceSegments.

The dashboard follows the same principle: with a logger attached it will show — for G-force rather than fall back to the phone's raw accelerometer, which would report the driver's hand instead of the car.
