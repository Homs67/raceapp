Overview

Motion, laps & G-force

The derived numbers drivers care about — cornering and braking G, lap times, live delta, 0–60 — and the algorithms that produce them. All of it lives in SessionKit as small, pure, sample-at-a-time types.

How these were built

Most parameters on this page weren't guessed. They were chosen by replaying a real recorded drive through the algorithm and scoring the output against an independent sensor. When you change one, do the same — the 2026-09-16 drive export is the reference dataset used so far.

Car-frame G

A sensor measures acceleration along its own axes. The phone sits in a cradle at an arbitrary angle; the RaceBox is plugged into a steeply raked OBD port. To show "braking 0.8 g" or "cornering 0.6 g left", the app has to learn, for each sensor, which way is down and which way is forward. That is calibration, and it produces car.latG (+ right) and car.longG (+ accelerating).

How alignment works

For each one-second window, compare the mean sensor acceleration (a 3-vector) with the change in GPS speed over the same second (a scalar, in g). Accumulate the normal equations and solve for the direction u that best predicts GPS acceleration — a small ridge term keeps straight-line driving from producing garbage perpendicular to the data — then project u into the horizontal plane leveling found. That is forward; right is forward × up.

The length of u before normalising is the fit scale. A correct fit on a correctly-scaled sensor gives about 1.0. A fit can correlate beautifully and still point the wrong way — scale is what catches it.

LoggerFrameTracker — three measured decisions

The RaceBox reports gravity and motion summed, so the tracker has to split them itself before reusing CarFrameCalibrator. Each of these was wrong in the first version and was found by measurement:

  1. Gravity is the negated filtered vector. An accelerometer reports specific force: +1 g up at rest. CoreMotion's gravity points down. Getting this wrong mirrors lateral G — caught because the correlation against GPS turn rate flipped sign.
  2. The gravity filter freezes while the car is manoeuvring. A plain moving average mistakes a long corner for gravity and subtracts it: a steady 0.8 g corner read 0.24 g after six seconds and 0.07 g after twelve. The estimate only updates while total specific force is within ±0.03 g of 1 g.
  3. The filter time constant is 30 s, not 5. Scored end to end with the validator's own metric, 5 s gave 0.766 (still marginal), 10 s 0.904, 30 s 0.996, then it plateaus. Only the freeze in step 2 makes a constant this long safe.

A restored frame is checked once against the first settled reading: if "down" has moved more than 25°, the device has been refitted and the frame is discarded. The threshold is loose on purpose — parking slope and road camber move "down" a few degrees on every drive.

Checking the result

  • GForceValidator runs at export: averages recorded car.longG over ~2 s GPS windows, searches 0–3 s of lag, and reports correlation, slope and implied misalignment. Verdict verified needs correlation ≥ 0.75 and slope in 0.8–1.25.
  • GCalibrationView (Settings → Calibrate G-force) is a guided version for the driver: hold still, then three straight-line accelerate-and-brake pulls, with a live count of good seconds and which requirement is still missing.

Laps

Tracks

Each track is a bundled JSON file (raceApp/Tracks/*.json) built from OpenStreetMap by tools/build_tracks.py: a centreline in driving order at ~4 m spacing, a start/finish gate, named corners. Bundled today: Laguna Seca, Big Willow, Streets of Willow. At session start the track is the demo's, or the nearest one to the current GPS fix.

The pipeline

SessionMetrics pumps bus snapshots at 5 Hz through:

TypeInput → output
LapTimerGPS fixes → crossings of the start/finish gate (direction-checked) → current, last and best lap.
TrackProgressA GPS fix → its distance along the lap, s, by projecting onto the nearest centreline segment in a window around the last match. Rejects fixes more than 30 m off the line (pit lane, off track).
LapDelta(s, elapsed) → time ahead/behind the session's best lap at the same point. The lap is divided into 5 m bins; every bin crossed between two fixes is interpolated, so 1 Hz phone GPS still fills them all.
SectorsThe lap split into equal thirds by distance; each completed sector holds its delta, the current one runs live.

Demo mode drives laps at varying pace (PacedLapSchedule) — a deterministic simulator would make every lap identical and the delta would read 0.00 forever.

Acceleration runs

DragMeter watches speed (OBD speed when available, else GPS), arms itself at a standstill, and records 0–60 mph, 0–100 mph and the quarter mile with trap speed. It keeps the current run and the session best. The Acceleration widget shows whichever the driver picks.

Gear and steering

  • Gear isn't on the bus — GearEstimator matches the speed/RPM ratio against the car's gearing table (8% tolerance), and GearTracker (owned by DashboardLiveFeed) adds the context: idling or crawling below 2 m/s is N, crawling with revs up is a 1st-gear launch even while the clutch slips, an unresolved ratio while moving keeps the previous gear for 2 s (a shift), and after that it is N at idle or the last gear otherwise. On the Oct 19 2026 drive this took the blank gear from 55% of the time to 1.4%. Reverse is a direction, not a ratio (on the ND2 its overall ratio sits where a slipping clutch in 2nd also lands), so DirectionSense calls it from physics: below 3 m/s, speed rising while calibrated car-frame longitudinal G points backwards means reversing; standing still for 1.5 s forgets the verdict. It needs a calibrated frame, so phone-only sessions never show R.
  • Steering is recorded as the steering wheel angle (can.steering, about ±480° on the ND). The widget divides by the rack ratio (15:1, a documented constant to confirm) to show the road-wheel angle, about ±32°.
